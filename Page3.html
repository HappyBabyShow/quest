Casa do Infante and the Mystery of the Royal Doors Casa do Infante is one of Porto’s oldest stone buildings. In 1325, King Afonso IV ordered the construction of the Royal Customs House (Alfândega Régia) here to collect duties from ships navigating the Douro River and to challenge the authority of the powerful bishop. Later, it housed the Royal Mint (Casa da Moeda), and in 1394, Prince Henry the Navigator was born within these walls. The Legend of the Three-Locked Door and Crown Treasures The building’s most intriguing lore surrounds its subterranean vaults and the former mint: * The Vault of Medieval Porto: Deep in the basement, behind massive granite archways and heavy oak doors reinforced with wrought iron, the crown kept bullion brought from overseas and newly struck royal coinage. * The Rule of Three Keys: According to tradition and old customs statutes, the main vault door was fitted with three distinct locks. The keys were never entrusted to a single individual: * The first key was kept by the Royal Treasurer (Almoxarife); * The second by the clerk of the mint; * The third by the Captain of the Guard. The door could never be opened in secret—the heavy bolt would yield only when all three turned their keys simultaneously. * The Counterfeiter’s Secret: Urban legend recounts that an apprentice at the mint once tried to fashion a wax impression of one of the keys to sneak through a hidden passage into the vault. His plot was exposed, and the counterfeit keys were sealed into the stonework of one of the door arches. Locals claim that anyone who touches the right stone along the doorway can still hear the faint clanking of coins. The Secret Beneath the Threshold: A Portal Through Time Major archaeological excavations beneath the complex revealed an even deeper secret: * Beyond the unassuming stone passageways below lies a literal descent into antiquity. Beneath the medieval flagstones, archaeologists unearthed the remains of a luxurious 4th-century Roman villa, featuring polychrome mosaics, water basins, and an underfloor heating system. * The cellar doorways of Casa do Infante do not merely lead into neighboring rooms—they bridge over 1,600 years of continuous history, stretching from Roman antiquity straight into the Age of Discovery. Quest Challenge: "The Mysterious Door" * Objective: Find an ancient stone archway or an iron-studded vault door within the building’s passageways. * Quest Question: Why did the main vault door require three different locks if armed guards watched the entrance around the clock? (Answer: To prevent any single official or guard from accessing the treasury alone—it could only be unlocked when all three keyholders were present).
