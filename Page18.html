How Wellington Liberated Porto Using a Few Boats — May 12, 1809 This is one of Porto's most gripping stories—how a handful of wine boats helped an entire army cross the river and launch a surprise attack against the French. 1. The French Believed the City Was Impregnable In March 1809, Marshal Soult’s army captured Porto. The French controlled the northern bank of the Douro, while the Anglo-Portuguese army under the command of Sir Arthur Wellesley, the future Duke of Wellington, approached from the south. The French had destroyed the pontoon bridge and gathered all available boats onto their side of the river. Soult was confident: without a bridge or boats, the enemy could not cross. 2. The Barber Who Helped Liberate the City On the morning of May 12, a local Portuguese barber told the British where the French had concealed several boats. With the help of local residents, Lieutenant Colonel John Waters managed to secure a few vessels. According to the prevailing account, they initially retrieved three or four large wine barges. Aboard these barges, British soldiers began secretly crossing the Douro, landing near the Seminary building, which the French had left virtually unguarded. 3. The French Discovered the Soldiers Too Late The vanguard British units occupied the seminary and turned it into a fortified defensive position. By the time the French noticed the enemy, the British were already firmly entrenched on the northern bank, and a fierce firefight broke out. Local residents rushed to assist their liberators, taking their own boats onto the river to ferry across wave after wave of soldiers. Marshal Soult realized he could not hold the city and ordered a general retreat. On May 12, 1809, Porto was liberated.
