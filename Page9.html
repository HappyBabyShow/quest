The Barredo Stairs — Escadas do Barredo: A Journey into Medieval Porto This is one of the most atmospheric corners of Porto, where you can truly step back into a medieval city. 1. A Thousand-Year-Old Pathway In the Middle Ages, Porto was divided into two distinct sections: the Upper City, centered around the Cathedral, and the Lower City along the Douro River, inhabited by merchants, fishermen, and craftsmen. The Barredo Stairs formed part of one of the oldest routes connecting the Cathedral to the riverfront. Merchants carried goods up these steps, residents descended toward the boats, and porters and artisans bustled back and forth. The passages were so steep and narrow that carts and carriages could not navigate them, giving rise to this network of granite staircases. 2. Why Are the Houses So Narrow and Tall? Look around: the buildings stand tightly packed, leaving barely enough room for sunlight to filter through. Land was scarce within the walled historic core, forcing structures to grow upward rather than outward. Workshops and taverns occupied the ground floors, while families lived in the upper stories. Today, you can still see laundry hanging across lines, tiny wrought-iron balconies, weathered doors, and timeworn window frames. This is no open-air museum—it remains a living, inhabited neighborhood. 3. Secrets, Smugglers, and Nightlife For centuries, Barredo was a gritty district of dockers, working-class families, sailors, and traders. Its narrow alleys, sharp corners, and labyrinthine stairs offered ideal cover. Historical records often note the area's harsh living conditions and association with prostitution and petty crime. One can easily imagine sailors and merchants slipping through the shadows after dark, trying to avoid notice, giving rise to local legends about hidden smuggler tunnels and secret storehouses. 4. The Legend of Truth and Lies Nearby lie the Escadas das Verdades (The Stairs of Truth). According to local lore, women would gather on these steps to wash clothes and discuss neighborhood news. Some shared actual facts, while others spun rumors. As stories traveled up and down the hill, fact blurred with fiction—leading locals to nickname the steps both the "Stairs of Truth" and the "Stairs of Lies."
