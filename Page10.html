São Bento Station is one of the most beautiful railway stations in the world. - Until the 16th century, the site was home to the São Bento da Avé-Maria monastery, founded in 1518. - In 1896, construction of the new station began on this site. - It was officially opened in 1916. The architect was José Marques da Silva. - Around 20,000 azulejo tiles adorn the main hall. They were created by the artist Jorge Colaço. The walls depict important events in Portuguese history: the meeting between King João I and Philippa of Lancaster, the conquest of Ceuta in 1415, rural life, folk festivals and the development of transport. Interesting fact: nuns used to pray on the site of the station, whereas today thousands of travellers pass through it every day.
